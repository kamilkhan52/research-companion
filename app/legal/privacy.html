You build a library of papers, discuss them with an AI model, listen to narrated explanations, and keep notes that carry forward into the next paper you read. The short version

  • We collect the email address you sign in with, and the research work you create in the app: your papers, your questions, your notes.
  • Your reading content is sent to AI providers so they can answer you and narrate. That is the product; it cannot work otherwise.
  • There is no advertising, no analytics SDK, no crash reporting, no tracking, and no advertising identifier. We do not sell your data and we do not use it to train models.
  • The app asks for no location, camera, microphone, contacts, or photo-library access.
  • You can delete your account and everything attached to it from inside the app, or by asking us.

What we collect

Your account

You sign in with Google, with an email address and password, or with a one-time link sent to your email address. We store the account identifier and email address that sign-in produces. If you set a password, it is stored by our authentication provider as a salted hash — we never see it, and neither the app nor our server can read it back. Sign-in itself is handled by Supabase Auth, our authentication provider; if you use Google, Google returns a profile to Supabase that may include your name and profile picture, and Supabase retains that identity record even though the app itself reads only your account identifier and email address.

Your research work

This is the substance of what we hold, and it is all content you create or choose:

  • project names, and the reading lists you build;
  • paper details you supply or we retrieve — titles, arXiv identifiers, DOIs, links;
  • PDFs you upload, and the text extracted from papers, kept so the model can read them with you;
  • the questions you ask, the model's answers, and the full transcript of each discussion, including drafts you retried;
  • notes and syntheses you approve, and their revision history;
  • topics you enter when you ask the app to discover new papers.

This material is stored on our server as a version-controlled repository per project — the history is the record, which is why edits and approvals are kept rather than overwritten.

Your settings

The model, voice, speech and prior-notes preferences you choose are stored on our server so they follow you between devices. Appearance choices — theme, density, sort order, playback settings, and unsent drafts — stay on your device and are never sent to us.

Operational records

When the app does something that costs money or runs in the background, we record what ran: the operation, the model used, token counts, cost, timestamps, and job identifiers. This is what usage limits and billing will be based on when paid plans begin. Our server also writes ordinary operational logs — request paths and status codes, project and paper identifiers, model and operation names, timings, error categories, and, when a sign-in is refused because the account is not on the tester list, that account's identifier.

Reports of AI content

If you report an AI response as offensive, we store your account identifier, a pointer to where the response was (project, paper, and position in the thread), the reason you selected, and any note you wrote. We deliberately do not copy the reported text into that record; it already exists in your project's history, and one copy that can be trusted is better than two that can disagree.

What we do not collect

No location, camera, microphone, contacts, call or SMS, photo library, or advertising identifier. The app's only Android permission is the one needed to show a playback notification. There is no advertising, analytics, attribution, or crash-reporting library in the app. When you attach a PDF, the app can read only the file you pick.

How we use it

To run the product and nothing else: to sign you in and keep your work yours; to generate discussion, narration, notes and paper suggestions; to retrieve papers you ask for; to remember your preferences; to measure usage against your plan; to keep the service working and secure; and to act on content reports. We do not profile you, we do not advertise to you, and we do not use your research work to train any model of our own.

About the AI providers specifically

This is the part worth reading twice, because it is where your research content actually goes. To answer a question about a paper, we must send that paper's text, your question, and the earlier discussion to a model provider. We reach those providers through OpenRouter, which forwards each request to a provider able to serve the model you selected.

We instruct OpenRouter to route only to providers that do not retain the content we send and do not train on it. That restriction is applied on every request. It limits which providers can serve a request, and a model with no such provider available will not run rather than fall back to one that retains data. We rely on those providers' own representations about retention, as any customer must.

That restriction covers the models that generate discussion, and nothing else. Being precise about the exceptions matters more than a tidy claim:

  • Narration. To read a passage aloud we send the narration text — written from the paper and your discussion — to OpenAI's speech service. We have no zero-retention arrangement there; it is subject to OpenAI's standard API terms, which retain inputs for a limited period for abuse monitoring.
  • Web search. If you ask the app to search or fetch a page, the search terms derived from your question go to the search provider under that provider's ordinary terms. Those terms are written by the model and routinely quote the paper.
  • Fetching a paper. The site you fetch sees an ordinary request from our server for that document.

Keeping it safe

The app connects to our server over HTTPS, and our server reaches the providers above over HTTPS. One exception, which we would rather state than paper over: when you ask us to fetch a paper from a site you have named, some academic hosts still offer only unencrypted http, and we fetch those as given rather than refusing. Nothing of yours travels on such a request beyond the address of the document you asked for. Your projects are readable only by your account: ownership is enforced in the database on every request, and a project you do not own is indistinguishable from one that does not exist. Narrated audio is stored per account rather than in a shared pool. Provider API keys are held by the operator of the service and are never readable by users. Provider error responses are stripped before they reach our logs, so prompts and paper text do not leak through error messages.

No service is perfectly secure, and this one is operated by one person. We have designed for the failures that matter — isolation between accounts, and deletion that actually completes — rather than claiming more than we can support.

How long we keep it

  • Your research work is kept until you delete it or delete your account. There is no automatic expiry: a reading library that quietly erased itself would be worse than useless.
  • Deleting a project moves it to a recoverable area rather than erasing it immediately, so an accident is fixable. That copy currently has no expiry — it stays until you delete your account, which erases it.
  • Generated audio is cached per account until your account is deleted.
  • Operational logs are held by our hosting provider and are not used to reconstruct your reading. We have not yet set a retention period for them; when we do, this page will say what it is.
  • After you delete your account we keep two content-free records: a pseudonymous entry noting that a deletion happened and when, and the bare identifiers of projects that once existed, so an identifier cannot be reused to reach data that a stale authorisation still refers to. Neither contains your reading, your notes, or your email address.

Deleting your account

In the app: Settings → Delete account. You will be asked to sign in again and to type a confirmation, because this cannot be undone. Deletion removes your projects and their history, recoverable copies of deleted projects, your notes and transcripts, your settings, your cached audio, your content reports, and finally your sign-in identity itself.

If you cannot reach the app, the same request can be made from our deletion page, which also lists exactly what is removed and what is kept.
